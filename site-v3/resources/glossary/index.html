Filtration & Paper Machine Clothing Glossary

The technical vocabulary of industrial filter media, forming wires, press cloths and dryer belts — in clear definitions.

Key Technical Terms (A–Z)

Air permeability (CFM)
The volume of air (cubic feet per minute) passed by one square foot of fabric at a standard pressure drop. The core specification for dryer and filter fabrics.
Anti-hydrolysis yarn
Polyester monofilament stabilised against hot-water attack — standard for humid forming sections and FGD duties.
Anti-static yarn
Conductive (usually carbon-cored) filament woven into the fabric to drain static charges on high-speed dryers.
Belt press filter
A continuous filter that squeezes sludge between two moving press filter belts through gravity, wedge and pressing zones.
Bullnose joint
A PTFE belt joint where folded, rounded belt ends overlap — quick to fit and re-lace.
CFM
See Air permeability. Typical spiral dryer fabrics reach ~900 CFM; filled press belts run 20–150 CFM.
Clipper seam
A metal clip (wire hook) seam joining belt ends — strong and repairable.
Disc filter
A rotary vacuum filter with segmented discs covered by filter fabric sectors.
Dryer fabric
The dryer fabric carrying paper, board or food through heated dryer sections; also called dryer belt.
FGD
Flue gas desulfurization in power plants — its gypsum by-product is dewatered on vacuum belt filters.
Filler yarns
Extra monofilaments inserted into spiral belt loops to raise density and lower permeability (1–5 yarns; round or flat section).
Filter press cloth
Press filter fabric (monofilament, multifilament, staple or combinations) cut and sewn for chamber and membrane filter plates.
Forming fabric
The wet-end forming fabric belt that forms and dewaters the fiber web; historically called forming wire (from the bronze-wire era).
Forming wire
Trade name for a forming fabric — still standard among machine crews and in B2B listings.
Fourdrinier
The classic flat-wire paper machine; the forming section is still often called the wire section.
Horizontal vacuum belt filter (HVBF)
A continuously indexing or endless-belt vacuum filter for dewatering and washing duties.
Mesh count
The number of openings per centimetre (or per inch) of a woven mesh — higher count, finer opening.
Monofilament
A single smooth yarn — best cake release and blinding resistance in filter cloths.
Multifilament
A yarn twisted from many filaments — finer retention, clearer filtrate, harder cake release.
Open area
The percentage of fabric surface that is opening; high open area = high flow.
Paper machine clothing (PMC)
The umbrella term for forming, press and dryer fabrics on a paper machine.
Pin seam
A joint where a pin (or clipper wire) runs through loops formed in the belt ends.
PPS
Polyphenylene sulphide — a monofilament/felt polymer for hot, chemical duty (~240 °C long-term in spiral fabrics).
PTFE
Polytetrafluoroethylene — non-stick, food-grade, 260 °C; used as belt coating and membrane material.
Scarfed joint
A PTFE belt joint planed to a taper so the overlap stays near belt thickness — smooth over rollers.
Screen mesh / sifter mesh
Woven mesh fitted as the working surface of a vibrating, gyratory or circular sifter.
Spiral dryer fabric
Spiral-link belt without filler yarns — open structure (up to ~900 CFM) for drying and conveying.
Spiral press filter belt
Spiral-link belt with 1–5 filler yarns — dense, low-permeability filter belt for belt press filters.
SSB (triple layer)
Sheet-side / machine-side triple-layer forming fabric construction for quality paper grades.
Square weave mesh
Plain-weave (1/1) polyester square mesh with precise square openings, e.g. 40–200 mesh count.
Tube sheet
The perforated plate of a baghouse that filter bags hang from — bags are sized to it.
Vacuum filter fabric
Vacuum filter fabric for vacuum belt, disc and drum filters, in PET, PP and PVDF.
